Coeliac disease: essentially excluded · no test needed

Coeliac disease needs HLA-DQ2.5 or DQ8 to happen at all, and more than 99 in 100 people who have it carry one of them. Your file read neither. A tissue transglutaminase test would come back negative and would not change this answer, so the plan does not order one.

Haemochromatosis: excluded · no test needed

The two variants that carry almost all of the risk — C282Y and H63D — are both absent. Your ferritin is 22 ng/mL, which is under the floor, so iron overload is not only unlikely on the genome, it is the opposite of what your blood is doing.

excluded● A
Type 2 diabetes: +40 % background risk from one TCF7L2 allele

One copy of the T allele, the strongest common variant anyone has found for type 2 diabetes. It changes how much insulin your pancreas releases after a meal, not how your muscles answer it. It moves the starting odds and nothing else — your HbA1c of 5.6 % and your missing fasting insulin decide the rest. Lifestyle erases most of a ×1.4.

×1.4● A
Insulin resistance: a small nudge, already overtaken

Two copies of the FTO A allele, which is the full dose of a small effect, and the effect is on appetite, not on metabolic rate. It moved the starting point by ×1.2 per copy. Your measured HbA1c has since passed it: a measurement always beats a starting point.

×1.2per A allele● B
Cardiovascular risk: the genome adds nothing

No APOE ε4 copy — you are ε2/ε3 — and you are a non-carrier at both LPA tag variants. Neither of the two rules that would have raised this condition fired. Your LDL of 131 mg/dL is doing all of the work here, which is why the plan aims at the LDL and not at the gene. One once-in-a-lifetime Lp(a) blood test would still settle Lp(a) properly: the tag variants only rule out the common high-risk haplotypes, not a raised Lp(a) from another cause.

no change● A

A variant shifts a starting point; your numbers decide the rest. If a marker and a variant disagree, the marker wins, because it is what your body is doing today. The two “excluded” cards are the exception, and they are exceptions for a reason: they are absences of something a disease cannot happen without.

Where the numbers come from

Real: the six calls — APOE ε2/ε3, LPA non-carrier at both tag variants, HFE neither C282Y nor H63D, HLA with no DQ2.5 and no DQ8, FTO A/A, TCF7L2 C/T — and the rsids behind each of them: rs429358, rs7412, rs10455872, rs3798220, rs1800562, rs1799945, rs2187668, rs7454108, rs660895, rs9939609, rs7903146. The catalogue itself is real: eleven rows over sixteen rsids, seven of which point at a condition and four of which point at none. The multipliers and their grades are the catalogue's own — TCF7L2 ×1.4 per T allele at grade A, FTO ×1.2 per A allele at grade B, APOE ×1.3 for an ε4 copy at grade A, LPA a likelihood ratio of 4, HFE 50 and 5. The markers on the cards are real: HbA1c 5.6 %, ferritin 22 ng/mL, LDL 131 mg/dL. The file itself was read Feb 3 2026.

Not on this page, and deliberately: MTHFR's genotype. It is the seventh catalogue row with a condition attached (folate deficiency, grade C), and the call for it is not among the values this mockup was built from, so the row is not drawn rather than guessed. The four note rows — CYP1A2, MCM6/LCT, G6PD, SLCO1B1 — are named without a genotype for the same reason; what is asserted about them is only what the catalogue says they do, which is nothing to any likelihood.

Build cost

Exists today. genome_variants stores rsid, genotype, chromosome, position and the uploadId, unique per user and rsid, and only ever holds rsids that are in the catalogue. lib/genome-catalog.ts holds the eleven GenomeRows, each with its rsids, grade, source, effect prose, a conditions: string[] array, and a call() that turns a genotype map into a GenomeCall with a plain-English meaning. lib/genome.ts sniffs a 23andMe or AncestryDNA export, parses it, and writes one profile fact per called row. components/genome-table.tsx already renders the gene list. Entry is POST /api/upload, which sniffs the file and dispatches to saveGenome; there is no /api/genome.

Does not exist. Everything above the table. One: a per-condition verdict. The only thing the engine computes today is movesAnything(row, call), a boolean that scans for a matching rule and then throws away which rule matched. There is no (row, call) → condition, direction, magnitude projection, so “Type 2 diabetes ×1.4” has nowhere to come from except the prose effect string. Two: an answer for an absence. When a row calls nothing, call() returns null, which is indistinguishable from “that rsid was not in the file”. The coeliac and haemochromatosis cards are both absences, and both need the catalogue to say “absent, and here is the negative likelihood ratio that follows”, which no row does. Three: a test recommendation. Nothing in the genome path emits “no test needed” or “order this test”; the tests-to-order block on Plan is fed by marker cadences, not by the genome. Four: the coverage line. “Eleven read, three moved something” needs the per-row result kept, and today the results are recomputed and discarded on every render.

What the engine must gain. A GenomeVerdict — {conditionId, direction: "up" | "down" | "none", factor, excluded: boolean, testNeeded: boolean, reason, grade, source, rows: GenomeRow[]} — produced by widening movesAnything from a boolean to the matched rule, plus a negative-likelihood field on the rows whose absence is the finding (hla_dq, hfe, lpa). It can stay a pure function over the catalogue and the stored variants: no new table, no new endpoint. The one thing that would need storage is the Home card's “3 of 11”, if it should survive without recomputing.
