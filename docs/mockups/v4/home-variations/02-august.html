Where the numbers come from

Real, from the test account: 52 markers with a value (26 optimal · 19 normal · 7 off), TPO 320 IU/mL, vitamin D 19 ng/mL, HbA1c 5.6 %, ALT 34 U/L, LDL 142 mg/dL, twelve systems with sex hormones never measured, draws on Dec 9 2025 (48 results), Feb 1 2026 (1), Aug 1 2026 (15) and Aug 31 2026 (1), 17 protocol items with 0 ticked today and a 5-day streak. Illustrative, with real shapes and units: the Apple Health day (8 412 steps, 34 min exercise, 7 h 12 sleep) and the resting-HR series — 90 daily values whose two printed numbers are the real ones the engine reports, 58 bpm thirty days ago and 55 today, against a 90-day mean of 58. The phone synced on 86 of the 90 days; the four gaps are Jun 17, Jul 4, Jul 26 and Aug 19.

August is good at making a health screen feel like weather rather than a lab result: soft amber, rose and sky blobs behind translucent glass, everything pill-shaped, big friendly numerals. The hue still obeys the ledger — amber because seven markers are off — so the warmth is never a lie. In the app it costs the most of the five: backdrop-filter on every tile, three blurred blobs behind the scroll view, and a contrast budget that has to be checked on every state (the same glass over a green background must keep the count line legible). On iOS it is the most native feeling — this is UIVisualEffectView and Liquid-Glass territory — but blur over a scrolling list is a real GPU cost on older phones, and Reduce Transparency has to fall back to flat tiles that still look intentional.
