Today is the row that matters. It is a WKWebView pointed at the website, with cookies pushed in before the load and harvested back out after every navigation. Making it native is not a styling job: it needs the two GET endpoints above that do not exist.

05

Where the numbers come from

Real: the 27 HealthKit types the phone reads and the five named on the Body frame — StepCount, RestingHeartRate, SleepAnalysis, AppleExerciseTime, DietaryProtein — with the units the phone sends so the server never converts; the two wire-only types the phone synthesises for workouts; the “seen, not used” list, which is a real stored key; the counters 26 optimal / 19 normal / 7 off; the twelve systems and their states, with sex hormones never measured; the Aug 1 2026 draw and its 52 markers; the protocol items and their adherence — selenium 86 %, vitamin D 100 %, sardines 0 % since Jun 14 2026; TPO 320 → under 100 IU/mL, vitamin D 19 → 40–60 ng/mL, ferritin 22 → above 50 ng/mL, HbA1c 5.6 %; the four capture buttons, which are the four things /api/capture and the composer actually accept; the meal pipeline itself — one vision call, per-item name, portion, kcal, protein, carbs and fat, then a server-side sum with a plausibility clamp, then four nutrition chips and a last-meal fact.

Illustrative: the meal. Four items, their portions and their macros — 422 kcal, 29 g protein, 31 g carbs, 20 g fat — are a plausible output of the vision call, not a logged one, and the card labels every one of them “est.” for exactly that reason. So are the day's other numbers on the Body and Today frames: 7 234 steps, 55 bpm, 6.8 h, 42 min, 88 g protein, the 2 190 days and 412 readings on the Settings counts, the 0.86 confidence, and the four types in “seen, not used”.

Build cost

Exists today. The whole write path. POST /api/sync/healthkit takes up to 20 000 samples of {type, unit, value, start, end, sourceBundle}, maps 27 types onto readings, daily logs and profile facts, and the Swift side batches on day boundaries, holds the incomplete newest day back, and only commits its anchor after the POST is accepted. GET /api/sync/healthkit/totals already answers the Settings screen. POST /api/capture already does the hard half of Meals: it takes the photo, classifies it, extracts per-item macros, sums them on the server with a clamp, and returns chips with an estimated flag; the JSON half writes them into the day's nutrition. Auth is a session cookie the Swift client adopts by hand. Capture and Settings are already native SwiftUI.

Does not exist. The read path. One: Today is a WKWebView of the website. There is no native Today, so there is nothing to restyle — it has to be built, and it needs a Today endpoint that returns the sentence, the counters, the systems and the three rail cards. GET /api/ledger is the closest thing and it is shaped for the web page. Two: there is no /api/body. The day list is computed by a page loader, not an endpoint. Three: there is no meal. A photo's items are summed and merged into the day's nutrition jsonb, and the items, the portions and the photo itself are not kept — so a Meals screen has nothing to list. It needs a meals table with the photo, the time, the items and the totals, and the estimated flag carried through. Four: the capture chip whitelist allows only supplements, medications, last-meal-hour and the four nutrition keys, so “this photo ticks your sardines item” is not a chip the confirm step will accept. Five: there is no Plan-for-today endpoint, and it cannot exist before the schedule columns do.

What the engine must gain. Three GET endpoints — /api/today, /api/body, /api/plan/today — each returning the same model its page already builds, so the work is extraction rather than new logic. One meals table (userId, at, photoKey, items jsonb, kcal, proteinG, carbsG, fatG, estimated, tickedItemId) and the file storage to hold the photo. One extra chip kind on the capture whitelist so a meal can tick a protocol item. On the Swift side: delete TodayView's web view and its cookie-harvesting, and add three native screens against those endpoints.
