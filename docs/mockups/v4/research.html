Where the numbers come from

Real, and already in the knowledge base: the four papers and their effect sizes and intervals — Gärtner 2002 (RCT, n = 36, −21 %, 95 % CI −34 to −7), Hollowell 2002 (NHANES III, n = 17 353, OR 8.4, 6.9 to 10.2), Vanderpump 1995 (cohort, n = 2 779, RR 3.0, 2.1 to 4.3) and Wang 2015 (meta-analysis, 20 sets, OR 1.6, 1.2 to 2.1), each with its A/B grade; Grant 2006 (Nat Genet) and Frayling 2007 (Science) on the pattern tab; the likelihoods 95 %, 97 %, 77 %, 76 % and 53 %; TSH 3.9 mIU/L against the lab's 0.4–4.5; HbA1c 5.6 % on Aug 1 2026, the same as on Dec 9 2025; TPO 320 IU/mL with a target under 100 by Feb 16 2027; selenium 200 µg started Jun 14 2026; added sugar cut on 24 of the last 30 days; the genome calls TCF7L2 C/T, FTO A/A and HLA with no DQ2.5 and no DQ8; the Aug 1 2026 run date and the 90-day cooldown, which is the real RESEARCH_COOLDOWN_DAYS.

Illustrative: the run counters on the Aug 1 receipt (212 hits, 40 read, 38 not retracted, 11 extracted, 4 proposed, 1 needs a look) — the shape is the real RunCounts, the values are a plausible run, not a logged one. The Sep 3 2026 “looked and found nothing” date on the two empty tiles is today's date, not a stored watch timestamp, because no watch timestamp is stored.

Build cost

Exists today. The research run itself: POST /api/hkb with {action: "research", conditionId} (app/api/hkb/route.ts), running researchRun from scripts/hkb-research.ts over Europe PMC (epmc() in lib/research.ts), with Semantic Scholar filling in abstracts and Crossref checking every DOI for a retraction. The papers themselves are already stored: hkb_evidence.paper and hkb_interventions.paper are both the HkbPaper jsonb — pmid, doi, title, year, journal, url, quote — beside a grade of A to E. watchWindow(lastRun, now) already builds a “published since” window and feeds it to the guideline query. hkb_import_runs and hkb_revisions record that a run happened. The /hkb activity tab already unions five tables at render time into one dated list.

Does not exist. Four things. One: a publication date. HkbPaper stores year only, and drops the abstract, the authors and the citation count before it writes, so “new since Aug 1” cannot be answered from the stored row — it needs published_at date and abstract text on the paper. Two: a per-person match. hkb_evidence.conditionId is global; the only per-user link is user_conditions. Deciding that a paper is “for you” means joining it to your conditions, your markers with a value, and your adopted protocol_items, and nothing does that join. Three: “what it would move”. buildLedger computes the change a rule makes, but the result is thrown away each request and only belief_snapshots ({p, state} per condition) survives. The line needs the scorer run twice, with and without the new rule, and the delta stored beside the paper. Four: a feed. There is no feed table and no seen/unseen state, and the research queue is a module-level in-process Set in lib/research.ts that dies on restart — the code's own comment says “when there are two [replicas], this becomes a table”. A nightly watch needs that table.

What the engine must gain. Two columns (hkb_evidence.paper.published_at, .abstract); one table, research_feed(userId, evidenceId, foundAt, movedConditionId, deltaP, seenAt); one queue table to replace the in-process Set; and one non-admin route, because /api/hkb is gated on isAdmin() and a user pressing “Research now” is not an admin. PubMed, OpenAlex and bioRxiv are each a new adapter beside epmc(); none of the three is referenced anywhere in the repo today.
