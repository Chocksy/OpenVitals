Where the numbers come from

Real, from the test account: 52 markers with a value (26 optimal · 19 normal · 7 off), TPO 320 IU/mL, vitamin D 19 ng/mL, HbA1c 5.6 %, ALT 34 U/L, LDL 142 mg/dL, twelve systems with sex hormones never measured, draws on Dec 9 2025 (48 results), Feb 1 2026 (1), Aug 1 2026 (15) and Aug 31 2026 (1), 17 protocol items with 0 ticked today and a 5-day streak. Illustrative, with real shapes and units: the Apple Health day (8 412 steps, 34 min exercise, 7 h 12 sleep) and the resting-HR series, 90 daily values whose two printed numbers are the real ones the engine reports, 58 bpm thirty days ago and 55 today, against a 90-day mean of 58. The phone synced on 86 of the 90 days; the four gaps are Jun 17, Jul 4, Jul 26 and Aug 19.

Board is good at holding a lot of numbers without noise. The black grid separates everything, so no panel needs a border, a shadow or a tint, and the eye lands on the violet 7 first, then the four triangles. Figures in a black grotesk read across a room, which suits a screen you glance at. In the app it costs very little: flat fills, square corners, two weights of rule, one accent. The real cost is layout discipline. Every cell has to claim a column span, so a new panel means re-cutting the row, and a cell with one short line looks underfed next to a 160 px numeral. Long marker names have to be clipped or the cells go ragged. On iOS the grid maps onto a two-column widget stack and lazy grids, and 2 px gutters hold up better than hairlines on a 3x screen. Archivo Black has to ship with the app, and the tight cells need a scrollable fallback at the largest Dynamic Type sizes.
