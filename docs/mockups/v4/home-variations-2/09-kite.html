Where the numbers come from

Real, from the test account: 52 markers with a value (26 optimal · 19 normal · 7 off), TPO 320 IU/mL, vitamin D 19 ng/mL, HbA1c 5.6 %, ALT 34 U/L, LDL 142 mg/dL, twelve systems with sex hormones never measured, draws on Dec 9 2025 (48 results), Feb 1 2026 (1), Aug 1 2026 (15) and Aug 31 2026 (1), 17 protocol items with 0 ticked today and a 5-day streak. Illustrative, with real shapes and units: the Apple Health day (8 412 steps, 34 min exercise, 7 h 12 sleep) and the resting-HR series — 90 daily values whose two printed numbers are the real ones the engine reports, 58 bpm thirty days ago and 55 today, against a 90-day mean of 58. The phone synced on 86 of the 90 days; the four gaps are Jun 17, Jul 4, Jul 26 and Aug 19.

Kite is good at making a long screen feel short. Ten cards live in one 176 px band instead of a grid four rows deep, the sentence gets the whole first rail at 40 px, and the cut-off card at the right edge tells you to swipe before any arrow does. One navy card carries the status, so the eye lands there first, and coral appears four times only, always with a triangle so it survives greyscale. In the app it costs a real scroll container: snap points, momentum on trackpads, keyboard access for every card, and a decision about what the rail shows when a system has no data. Horizontal rails also hide content from search and from screenshots, so the panel below has to repeat the full list of twelve. On iOS it is a plain horizontal ScrollView with paging off and snapping on, which UIKit and SwiftUI both give you; the risk is gesture conflict with the back swipe at the left edge, and Dynamic Type at the larger sizes breaks a fixed 7:4 card, so the card height has to grow with the text rather than stay locked to the ratio.
