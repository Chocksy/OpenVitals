Where the numbers come from

Real, from the test account: 52 markers with a value (26 optimal · 19 normal · 7 off), TPO 320 IU/mL, vitamin D 19 ng/mL, HbA1c 5.6 %, ALT 34 U/L, LDL 142 mg/dL, twelve systems with sex hormones never measured, draws on Dec 9 2025 (48 results), Feb 1 2026 (1), Aug 1 2026 (15) and Aug 31 2026 (1), 17 protocol items with 0 ticked today and a 5-day streak. Illustrative, with real shapes and units: the Apple Health day (8 412 steps, 34 min exercise, 7 h 12 sleep) and the resting-HR series, 90 daily values whose two printed numbers are the real ones the engine reports, 58 bpm thirty days ago and 55 today, against a 90-day mean of 58. The phone synced on 86 of the 90 days; the four gaps are Jun 17, Jul 4, Jul 26 and Aug 19.

Seismo is good at putting the trace first. Every panel is a channel on one roll of paper, so the eye tracks left to right and the resting-HR line gets 176 px of height instead of a thumbnail. The graph paper gives free alignment, the rail names the channel without spending a headline, and red ink stays rare: the pen line, the four off markers, the 7. In the app it costs very little to draw, no shadows, no radii, no gradients, two type families and four rules, but it costs restraint. The rail eats 55 px on every row, long system names need truncation, and a screen with nothing wrong looks like blank paper. On iOS it maps onto a plain grouped list with a leading label column; the condensed and mono faces both ship in the app bundle, the 1 px hairlines want hairline handling on 3x screens, and the rail must fold away under Dynamic Type before the numbers do.
